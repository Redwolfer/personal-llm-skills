# Research before positions

Both QUICK and DEBATE start with research. Complete this step before writing member briefs or positions. Use the browsing tools available in the current session and follow any limits the user places on research.

## Search across source types

Identify the decision's uncertain facts, competing approaches, and relevant failure modes. For factual topics, search beyond a single website or source category:

| Source type | What to look for | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Primary sources | Official documentation, original research, product announcements, public datasets, or an organization's own statement | Establish technical behavior, dates, figures, and stated policy. Read the conditions and version scope. |
| Reddit | Relevant communities, experience reports, recurring complaints, and counterexamples | Find practical concerns and leads to verify. A post reports a user's experience; it does not establish prevalence. |
| X/Twitter | Original public posts, author announcements, practitioner discussions, and links to underlying material | Check the original author, date, context, and linked evidence. Reposts and repeated claims are not independent confirmation. |
| Other relevant websites | Specialist forums, issue trackers, community discussions, independent reporting, and technical write-ups | Fill gaps and compare experiences. Follow citations back to the original evidence when possible. |

For researchable topics, attempt primary-source, Reddit, and X/Twitter searches by default. Use topic terms with a site filter where supported, such as `site:reddit.com`, `site:x.com`, or `site:twitter.com`. Add other websites when they can answer an unresolved question. These searches are queries for real public material; never invent a post URL or a summary.

QUICK starts with a small set of targeted queries covering these source types. DEBATE adds searches where evidence conflicts or a perspective lacks support. Query count is not the goal. Seek at least two distinct source domains for researchable claims when available, and explain any gap instead of filling the table with irrelevant results.

## Read and verify

1. Open the relevant results and read the material supporting each finding. A search snippet alone does not establish a claim.
2. Check who published it, when it was published, the event or version it describes, and any limitations that matter to the decision.
3. Prefer primary sources for technical, medical, legal, or financial facts. Community posts can identify questions or experiences to investigate; do not use them alone to establish those facts.
4. Keep credible contradictory findings and explain their different scope. Distinguish facts, the author's opinion, anecdotes, and the council's inference.
5. Cite the actual page or public post with a descriptive Markdown link. Preserve uncertainty and avoid unsupported quotations or claims about how many people agree.

Do not claim to have searched or read a site unless the tool results support that claim. If X/Twitter, Reddit, or another page is blocked, login-only, deleted, or absent from useful results, record that specific limitation. Use accessible evidence from other sites and leave unsupported claims unresolved. Do not treat a failed search as proof that no evidence exists.

## Build the research table

Use the schema in [OutputFormat.md](OutputFormat.md). Each finding has a row number, source link, source type, concrete finding, and its relevance to the council. Include the publication or event date in the finding when recency affects the decision.

Add clearly labeled status rows for the actual execution mode and any source-access limitations. Status rows are not research evidence. Assign relevant finding rows to each member's brief. If a member needs an additional fact, research it before using it in a later round.

Every factual claim in the debate must trace to a finding row or a fact supplied by the user. Mark inferences as inferences and unverified knowledge as unverified. Carry these qualifications through Humanizer and Gen Z styling. A source appearing in the table does not support claims outside its actual scope.

## Sessions without browsing

Keep the research table. State that no live search was performed and that Reddit, X/Twitter, and other sites were not checked in this session. Use supplied sources or facts where available. Label existing model knowledge as unverified and do not present changing facts as current. Ask for source material when the decision depends on evidence the session cannot obtain.

For a pure preference question without researchable facts, explain that in a status row and compare the user's stated criteria. Do not add unrelated web sources to make the table look researched.
